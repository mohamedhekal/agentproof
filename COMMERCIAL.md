# Commercial use and the future of AgentProof

## The short version

- **AgentProof is open source under the MIT License, and you may use it commercially** (inside a company, in a product, in a paid service) without asking and without paying, as long as you keep the copyright notice and the license text.
- **There is no commercial product today.** No customers, no pricing, no paid tier, no hosted service, no revenue. Nothing in this repository is an offer to sell anything. (The pitch deck's business-model slide is labelled a *proposed* model, not traction.)
- The maintainer **intends** to keep this repository, the public core, a genuinely useful MIT-licensed framework.

## What may come

The maintainer is exploring optional commercial offerings built **around** the open core, for example a hosted evaluation service, team dashboards, organization and access-control features, enterprise audit trails, and private integrations. If they are built:

- they would live in **separate, private repositories** that depend on the public core through its documented, versioned interfaces (see [docs/OPEN_CORE_ARCHITECTURE.md](docs/OPEN_CORE_ARCHITECTURE.md));
- the public core would **not** be crippled to push users toward them: capabilities that are in the public repository today stay in it;
- code that is already published under MIT stays MIT for everyone who received it. It cannot be re-labelled as proprietary after the fact, and nothing here tries to.

## What this means for you

| you are... | what to know |

| building a product on top of it | allowed; keep the MIT notice; do not imply endorsement or use the name as your own brand ([TRADEMARKS.md](TRADEMARKS.md)) |
| interested in a managed or enterprise version | none exists yet; to register interest, open an issue or email **mohamed.k.hekal@gmail.com** |

## Honest limits of this document

- It states intent, not a legal commitment, and it is not a contract or a licensing offer. A formal commercial license, terms of service, a privacy policy and any enterprise agreement would be separate documents prepared with legal advice.
- Future versions of the code could in principle be released under a different license by the copyright holder; copies already published under MIT would keep their MIT terms. Contributions accepted under MIT remain MIT. If that ever changes it will be announced in advance, and not applied to prior contributions.
