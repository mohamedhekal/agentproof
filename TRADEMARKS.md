# Trademarks and name usage

> **Status: unregistered, unreviewed, a proposal.** "AgentProof" is **not** a registered trademark. No trademark search or clearance has been done, and this document is **not legal advice**. Professional review (a trademark clearance search and advice from a qualified attorney in the relevant jurisdictions) is required before anyone asserts exclusive rights in the name or logo. Until then, treat the rules below as the maintainer's good-faith request, not as an enforceable legal claim.

## Copyright and trademark are different

The MIT License covers the **code and documentation**. It does not say anything about the name "AgentProof" or any logo, and it does not take away your right to copy, modify and redistribute the MIT-licensed code. This policy only concerns how the **name and mark** are used to identify a product or service.

## Proposed policy (conservative)

You are welcome to:

- say that your tool, paper, benchmark or product **uses, works with, extends or is compatible with** AgentProof ("compatible with AgentProof", "built on AgentProof");
- refer to the project by name in documentation, articles, talks and citations;
- distribute unmodified copies, and modified copies **with the MIT notice kept**, under a clear statement of what you changed.

Please do not:

- name a product, company, domain, package or social account "AgentProof" (or something confusingly similar) in a way that suggests it **is** the official project or is **endorsed** by the maintainer;
- use the project's logo or the deck's visual identity as your own branding;
- sell a modified version under the AgentProof name while implying it is the original. If you distribute a materially modified fork as a separate product, please choose a different name.

## The logo and deck

The shield mark and the pitch-deck artwork are **not** granted for trademark use by this document, and their copyright provenance is still to be confirmed (see [COPYRIGHT.md](COPYRIGHT.md)). Use plain text "AgentProof" until a logo policy exists.

## Before asserting exclusive rights (human actions)

1. Run a trademark clearance search (national registers and the WIPO Global Brand Database) for "AgentProof" in the classes and countries that matter to you.
2. Check for existing products, companies, domains and package names (for example on PyPI and npm) that could conflict.
3. Take advice on whether to register, and where. Unregistered ("common-law") rights, where they exist, depend on actual commercial use and on the jurisdiction; one day of public availability is unlikely to establish much.
4. Only after that, add a `™` or `®` notice. Do **not** use `®` unless the mark is actually registered.
5. If a commercial product ships under the name, decide who owns the mark (you personally, or a company) and record it.
